# Taurus, late October 2026 — research notes

Background for the three options behind `/plans/taurus-2026`. The brief: six or
seven days, roughly 100 km and 2,000 m a day, start in Antalya, finish around
Mersin or earlier in that direction. Road bike, same shape as the 2026 French
Alps traverse rather than the Morocco gravel options.

## The line

Between Antalya and Mersin the Taurus runs parallel to the coast, so an eastward
crossing is really a choice of which shelf to ride: the D400 at sea level, the
Taşeli plateau at 1,200–1,800 m, or the Konya plateau edge behind it. All three
options here use the plateau; they differ in how much coast they take on the way.

The corridor is stitched from these legs, with the road distances the research
turned up:

| Leg | km | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Antalya → Manavgat | 75 | Flat D400 across the Pamphylian plain |
| Manavgat → Akseki | 60 | D695 turns inland, climbs to 1,050 m |
| Akseki → Seydişehir | 66 | Over **Alacabel Geçidi, 1,825 m** — high point of the Antalya–Konya road |
| Seydişehir → Bozkır | 55 | Plateau edge, both towns ~1,125 m |
| Bozkır → Hadim | 50 | Down into the Göksu gorge at Yerköprü, then 700 m up; Hadim 1,530 m |
| Hadim → Taşkent | 10 | Taşkent sits ~7 km south-east of Hadim |
| Taşkent → Sarıveliler | 45 | Over the Konya/Karaman provincial crest, ~1,850 m |
| Sarıveliler → Ermenek | 42 | Sarıveliler averages 1,650 m; Ermenek 1,250 m |
| Ermenek → Mut | 86 | Ermenek dam gorge, then off the plateau to Mut at 275 m |
| Ermenek → Anamur | 80–100 | The alternative: ~2,000 m to sea level, hairpins throughout |
| Anamur → Silifke | ~130 | D400 headlands; new tunnels and viaducts since 2023 |
| Mut → Silifke | 76 | The whole Göksu canyon, downhill |
| Silifke → Uzuncaburç | 30 | 46 km of asphalt serves the plateau villages; Uzuncaburç 1,210 m |
| Kırobası (Mara) → Silifke | 45 | Kırobası is 125 km from Mersin by road |
| Erdemli → Güzeloluk | 34.5 | The escarpment road, sea level to ~1,400 m |
| Güzeloluk → Arslanköy | ~35 | Yayla road along the Bolkar shelf; Arslanköy ~1,475 m |
| Arslanköy → Mersin | ~45 | Via Kuzucubelen; the known Mersin–Kuzucubelen–Arslanköy loop is 121 km / 2,815 m |

Two passes are worth naming. **Alacabel** (1,825 m), between Akseki and
Seydişehir, is the highest point of the D695 and the one climb every option in
the region is built around. **Toros Geçidi** (2,215 m), on the Erdemli–Ayrancı
road above Güzeloluk, is higher still — but it leads north to Karaman rather than
east to Mersin, so it only fits as an out-and-back and none of the options take
it. Sertavul (1,664 m), on the Karaman–Mut D715, is likewise off the eastward
line.

## The three options

- **Taşeli traverse**, 7 days, 726 km / 11,998 m. Inland the whole way. The
  short third day (62 km) is deliberate: it is the day you drop into the Göksu
  gorge and climb straight back out to Hadim.
- **Coast & cols**, 7 days, 732 km / 12,423 m. Half the nights at sea level.
  Buys the two best descents in the region — Ermenek to Anamur, and Arslanköy to
  Mersin — at the price of a 2,750 m day out of Alanya.
- **The six-day line**, 6 days, 726 km / 11,677 m. The Taşeli traverse with a
  day removed; 121 km and 1,946 m a day, with a 2,550 m day and a 128 km day
  back to back.

Any of them can be stopped early: Silifke is the natural cut (a day and a half
short of Mersin), with buses onward and Adana airport about two hours away.

## Late October

- **Coast.** Antalya in October averages 26.6 °C by day and 15.2 °C at night,
  with 68 mm of rain across about 5.8 days. The sea is around 24.8 °C — still
  swimmable at Kızkalesi or Anamur.
- **Plateau.** Karaman, the nearest long-record station to Ermenek, sees daily
  highs fall from about 23 °C to 15 °C across the month, on roughly 19 mm of
  rain. Nights at 1,500–1,650 m (Hadim, Sarıveliler, Arslanköy) will be close to
  freezing by the end of October. Pack for a 25 °C start and a 2 °C morning.
- **Snow.** The Taşeli and Konya plateaus normally take their first snow in
  November; Sertavul and Alacabel close only occasionally, in deep winter. Late
  October is the last comfortable week, not a gamble — but an early front is the
  one thing that could shut the high crossings, and the coast option is the
  fallback if it does.
- **Daylight.** Türkiye stays on UTC+3 year-round. Sunset in Antalya is about
  18:40 on 1 October and just after 17:00 by the 31st, so the seven-hour days
  need an early start; the 121 km / 128 km days in the six-day option need a very
  early one.
- **Republic Day, 29 October**, falls on the Thursday of a 24–30 October trip and
  runs into the weekend. Domestic travel peaks and the mountain towns — which
  have one hotel each — fill up. Either book Ermenek, Mut and Arslanköy well
  ahead, or shift the week to 17–23 October.

## Beds

Reliable: Antalya, Alanya, Anamur, Silifke, Erdemli/Kızkalesi, Mersin — all with
plenty of choice. Akseki has small hotels; the restored *düğmeli ev* guesthouses
at Ormana, 12 km off-route, are the nicer stop. Hadim has an 80-room hotel opened
in 2021. Ermenek has the 72-room Selçuklu Otel and little else. Mut and Bozkır
have basic town hotels.

Thin, and worth a phone call before committing: **Sarıveliler** (the six-day
option's third night — Ermenek is 42 km further if it falls through),
**Arslanköy** (dağ evi accommodation exists at about 1,580 m) and **Kırobası**,
which is a yayla settlement rather than a town.

## Getting there and back

Turkish Airlines is carrying bicycles free on flights into both Antalya and
Adana through 31 December 2026, which fits the route exactly: in at Antalya, out
at Adana, with Mersin an hour from Adana by train. Intercity buses will normally
take a bike in the hold — front wheel out, and ask the company first, since
policies differ.

## What the committed tracks are, and are not

The session that did this research had no egress to a routing engine
(`brouter.de` was refused by the network policy), so the GPX under
`public/gpx/taurus-*/` are **sketches**, marked `"routeQuality":
"planned-sketch"` and labelled as such in the UI. What is real: the corridor, the
towns, the passes and their altitudes, the day splits, and the per-day distance
and climbing in the table above. What is drawn rather than surveyed: the line
between one named place and the next.

`scripts/taurus-route.json` holds the researched control points, and
`scripts/build-taurus-gpx.mjs` builds the tracks from them two ways:

```bash
npm run gpx:taurus                 # sketch — densify, bend and ripple to the researched targets
npm run gpx:taurus -- --brouter    # re-route every leg over OSM, then npm run gpx:stats
```

Run the second one as soon as a routing engine is reachable. It writes the same
files, and the numbers it produces are measurements rather than estimates.
